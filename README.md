# Arcane Tome

An animated, auto-calculating character sheet for D&D 5e (2024 rules). Every number on it can be rolled. Spells and attacks play an effect and a sound that match their damage type.

## What it does

- **Stats in, bonuses out.** You enter ability scores (tap **Edit** under the Abilities panel for − and + on each, then **Play** to lock them again), class levels, proficiencies and armor. The sheet works out the rest: modifiers, proficiency bonus, saves, all 18 skills (including expertise and Jack of All Trades), passive scores, initiative, AC (armor, shields, Unarmored Defense, Mage Armor), max HP, hit dice, spell save DC, spell attack, spell slots (single class, multiclass and Pact Magic), carrying capacity and jump distances. 2024 Exhaustion (−2 per level to d20 Tests, −5 ft Speed per level) is applied everywhere. Tap **Show the math** to see where each number comes from.
- **Dice roller on everything.** Checks, saves, skills, initiative, attacks, damage, spells, hit dice and death saves all roll. A floating chip sets Advantage/Disadvantage for the next d20. Set a target AC in the Combat panel and the sheet calls HIT or MISS; without one it says **HIT?** and leaves the call to your DM, rather than assuming a hit. Critical hits double the damage dice.
- **Fair dice, and proof.** Every die comes from the device's cryptographic random generator (`crypto.getRandomValues`), with rejection sampling so each face is exactly equally likely (a plain `x % sides` would favour low faces slightly). **Settings → Are the dice fair?** rolls any die thousands of times, draws the spread, and runs a Pearson chi-square goodness-of-fit test on it. A hit-chance calculator shows the real odds of an attack against an AC, with Advantage or Disadvantage.
- **Rolling dice.** Every roll throws dice that tumble and bounce on a velvet tray, then settle on the result before the card and the spell effect appear. Damage dice take their damage type's colour. A natural 20 ends in a critical moment themed to the action (fire for Fire Bolt, gold for a skill check); a natural 1 cracks the die.
- **Hit by hit.** Attacks and spells count up on the tray. A sword, axe, rapier, spear, hammer, fist or arrow (picked from the weapon) strikes the d20, then each bonus (STR, PROF, magic), then the total and HIT, MISS or CRITICAL. The damage dice are thrown next, and the weapon strikes once per die. A spell's dice roll as orbs of its element (fireballs for Fireball) and burst one by one. Tap anywhere to skip to the result. It can be turned off, or sped up, in Settings.
- **Enemies.** An Enemies page (right after Combat) lists the foes as the DM describes them, one line each: type them in (a name, how many, and any AC or HP you've been told), or pick them from the creature list with their stats hidden so nothing is spoiled. Tap one and it becomes your target, outlined in gold; your weapons, spells and companions' attacks then go at it, from its card or from the **Target** strip above Attacks, Spellcasting and Companions. Each attack uses what you know of its AC: when it's unknown you tap **Hit** or **Miss** once the DM says, and every answer narrows it down ("AC 15–17") until it's known. Its conditions change your rolls as the 2024 rules say: Advantage against a Blinded, Paralyzed, Petrified, Restrained, Stunned or Unconscious foe, against a Prone one within 5 feet (Disadvantage from farther), Disadvantage against an Invisible one, and automatic critical hits within 5 feet of a Paralyzed or Unconscious one. Your marks on it (Hunter's Mark, Hex, cast at it or added by hand, moved when it drops) add their dice to your hits on it and no one else. Casting a spell asks who it's at, starting with your target, and an area spell can take several; save spells wait for each enemy's **Failed** or **Saved**, unless it can't succeed. Damage is tallied on each enemy (you, each companion, each party member with **+ Party damage**), after any resistances you've noted; how hurt it looks is yours to set, or worked out (Bloodied at half) once you know its HP. A roll sent to the wrong enemy can be moved to the right one.
- **Made for the table.** **↶ Undo** in the top bar takes back the last change (a wrong HP, damage on the wrong enemy, a mistapped condition), or several in turn; a quick burst of changes, like a spell that spends a slot and marks an enemy, is one step. In a fight, a strip at the top of every page shows the round and whose turn it is, your Action, Bonus Action and Reaction (crossed out once used) and your target. When an enemy drops, your target moves to the next one standing, and if your Hunter's Mark or Hex was on it, a banner offers to move it (a Bonus Action). Free casts from class features, like a Ranger's Favored Enemy Hunter's Marks, appear in the spell's Cast menu with their uses left, as feat free casts do. The screen stays on while Initiative is running, a natural 20 or 1 gives a buzz on phones that can vibrate (both can be turned off in Settings), and the roll history is kept between visits.
- **Counters.** A Counters page (after Enemies) for anything you keep count of: arrows, gold, ki points, days on the road. Each is one line with its name and number (type over it to set it), and − and + around an amount box: +1 by default, or +37 when you type 37. **Edit** renames it, sets the number **Reset** goes back to, or removes it. Counters are saved with the character.
- **Concentration.** Taking damage while concentrating offers **Roll concentration save** at the right DC (10, or half the damage, up to 30) with your save bonus and conditions; failing it ends the spell and takes its marks off your enemies. Becoming Incapacitated or dropping to 0 HP ends concentration too, and casting another concentration spell replaces the last.
- **Initiative.** **Start a fight** on the Combat page: roll or enter your initiative and the enemies' and party's, and everyone is put in order, your companions right after you. **Next turn** moves down the list, skips enemies that are down, counts rounds, and starts your turn (and your companions') over when it comes back to you. Enemies who join mid-fight are slotted in by their initiative.
- **Conditions and effects.** Toggle the 15 conditions and track Exhaustion and timed effects (Bless, Bane, Guidance, Haste, Shield, Shield of Faith, or your own). They apply themselves: Poisoned gives Disadvantage on attacks and checks, Paralyzed fails Strength and Dexterity saves, Bless adds 1d4, Advantage and Disadvantage cancel out, rounds count down each turn. Each condition takes hold with its own animation and sound (stone cracking for Petrified, drifting Zs for Unconscious, green bubbles for Poisoned, hearts for Charmed, circling stars for Stunned, chains or vines for Grappled and Restrained, darkness for Blinded…), and lifts with a fading light; timed effects play their spell's look. Companions' conditions do the same. Settings → Conditions previews them all.
- **Import a character.** Read a sheet from a PDF (including fillable sheets and D&D Beyond exports), a Word or Google Doc (.docx), OpenDocument, text, web page or RTF, or paste what another AI wrote. Every page and every filled-in field is read: numbered weapon, equipment and spell rows, features and traits (bullets, D&D Beyond's `=== HEADINGS ===`), proficiencies and languages. Spells are matched against the full SRD and your rules library, and spells under each level heading get that level. Anything listed twice (on two pages, or as a trait and an action) comes in once. Magic weapons keep their full name ("Dragon Wing Shortbow"), with their base weapon's stats, the extra damage the sheet lists, and a magic bonus worked out from the sheet's attack bonus. You review what was found before anything is created. Mismatches (an AC or HP the math doesn't reach) are kept as overrides and pointed out. Everything is read on the device. Scans and photos aren't supported, because they would need text recognition. For unusual sheets there is a prompt to have an AI convert them first.
- **Equipment and magic items.** Inventory → **Item list** and Attacks → **More…** search the SRD 5.2.1's weapons, armor, gear, tools and magic items, plus any in your rules library. A magic weapon built on a base ("Flame Tongue", "+1 weapon") lets you pick the base, and comes with its extra damage and bonus. Armor can be worn straight away. **Paste** a weapon's or item's stat block, from a book, D&D Beyond or your own notes, and its damage, extra damage, properties, mastery, range, bonus, rarity and description land in the right fields. A weapon you already have can be filled in the same way. A pasted item can be saved to your item library as it goes in, and any weapon in Attacks can be saved there with **Save to my item library**, so it can be added again, exactly as you set it up, or given to another character or a companion. Each inventory item opens (▸) to its description and your notes.
- **Languages.** A page of its own lists every language you know, with its origin (from the SRD 5.2.1) and a short lore note on who speaks it and its character. Add several at once, invent your own and describe them, or replace any description to fit your world. The Character panel shows them too.
- **Slide between pages.** Swipe sideways on a phone or tablet, or two-finger swipe sideways on a laptop's trackpad (Mac or PC), to move to the next or previous page; the arrow keys do the same on the tabs. Fields, sliders, sideways-scrolling tables and Arrange keep their own gestures.
- **Heroic Inspiration slot** in At a glance and Vitals: tap the star when you gain it, tap again to spend it (reroll one die you just rolled and use the new roll). While you have it, ★ Inspiration shows at the top of every page.
- **Back button.** On Android, Back closes the dialog on top, then leaves Arrange, then goes back through the pages you visited; with nothing left it puts the app in the background rather than closing it. Escape closes dialogs on a computer.
- **Your layout and pages.** Fold any panel away. Under **Arrange**, drag panels to new places or columns, drag the gap between columns to resize them, and drag a panel's bottom edge to set its height. Each panel's **On:** chooser moves it to another page, or puts it on several (Attacks on Stats as well as Combat, say). **Pages…** adds pages of your own (for homebrew rules or another game), renames and reorders them; on a phone they're tabs, and on a wider screen the pages you made get tabs beside the main sheet. **Remove** takes a tile off every page you don't want it on; nothing in it is deleted, and it waits under **Pages… → Removed tiles** (or the Arrange bar, or an empty page) to add back in its old place. Every drag also has an arrow or button, so it works by keyboard and on phones (WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.7). The layout is kept per device; resetting it keeps the pages you made and brings removed tiles back.
- **Tiles of your own.** **New tile** makes a panel with notes, trackers (current and maximum, with − and +, such as Sanity or Corruption) and roll buttons that take any dice formula with `{STR}`, `{PB}` and the like. Tiles belong to the character, so they come along in backups.
- **Portrait.** Add a picture of your character. It's shrunk on the device, to about 320 px, before it's saved.
- **Dice page.** Build a pool from d4–d100 with a modifier, type any notation (`4d6kh3`, `2d20kl1`, `8d10!` exploding, `6d10>=8` successes), save your own dice per character (a roll like `3d6+{DEX}`, or a die with custom faces like Sun / Rain / Storm), and see the full roll history.
- **Level up, level down.** **Level up** walks you through the next level: which class (multiclassing checks the 13-in-the-main-ability rule, with a house-rule override), hit points (average or roll), subclass, Ability Score Improvement or a feat, fighting style, Epic Boon. It shows what changes first: new features, spell slots, cantrips and prepared spells. Then the whole sheet updates, with class and subclass features (their uses and dice by level) from the SRD 5.2.1, saves and training for a new class, and HP. **Undo last level** puts everything back. Features you edited by hand are left alone.
- **Companions.** Each one is a small character sheet: AC, HP with damage and healing, abilities, saves and skills, and every action as a button that rolls with the same hit-by-hit strikes as your attacks. It takes its own turn (Action, Bonus Action and Reaction used, movement against its speed, Dash), its actions spend that turn, and your New turn starts its turn over too. It has its own conditions, which shape its rolls the way yours do (Poisoned gives Disadvantage, Paralyzed fails Strength and Dexterity saves, a warning when it can't act). Give it gear from the item list or a pasted stat block: weapons it wields roll with its own Strength or Dexterity and Proficiency Bonus, armor or barding it wears sets its AC, and what it carries is weighed against its capacity for its size. Add one from the SRD creatures, paste a stat block (2014 or 2024 layout), or build one. Numbers can follow you as you level, such as `5 + 5 × {LEVEL:ranger}` HP or `{SPELLATK}` to hit, and any action's dice can step up at levels you set.
- **Reactions that add to a hit.** A companion reaction that adds damage to someone else's hit, like a Drakewarden drake's Infused Strikes (an extra 1d6 of its Draconic Essence type), is offered as a button on the result of each weapon hit, yours or another companion's. Its element (a drake's Draconic Essence: acid, cold, fire, lightning, poison, or any other type) is picked right on the companion's card. Tap it and its dice are rolled into that hit, doubled on a critical hit, and the companion's reaction is spent; if the reaction is already used, or the companion is incapacitated or at 0 HP, the button says so. It's read from the reaction's own text (a pasted or imported stat block), so nothing needs setting up beyond choosing its damage type.
- **Rules library.** Import rulebooks, supplements or your own game's rules (PDF, Word, text, web page). The text is kept on the device, split by its headings, and searchable. Classes (features by level, hit die, saves, subclasses), feats, spells, creatures and magic items found in it join the official ones in Level up, Add a spell, Add a companion and the item list. You review what was found before it is saved. The reader works from layout and wording, so it misses unusual formats: for those, there is a prompt to have an AI convert the document to the library's JSON format, which is validated on import. Packs can be turned off, exported, shared and are included in backups.
- **Spells.** Add any spell from the SRD 5.2.1 or your rules library, filtered by your classes (or any class), level and school, with the full description; or write your own. **Remove spells** puts a Remove button on each spell. The panel counts your cantrips and prepared spells against what your level allows.
- **Import spells.** Paste spells or upload a document (PDF, Word, text) under Add spell → **Import spells** or in the Spell compendium. Every spell in it is read (book layouts from either edition, D&D Beyond copies, plain notes), you check them and give a class to any that don't name one, and they go into your rules library, filed under their classes, so they show up in the spell list for those classes. For unusual layouts there's a prompt to have an AI convert them first.
- **Library tab.** The spell, feat and background lists live on their own page, **Library**, so the sheet itself holds only what your character has; on a wide screen the Library has its own tab beside the main sheet. A new character looks things up there and attaches them.
- **Spell compendium** (on the Library). Browse every spell the app knows, yours included, by level, class, school and source: read what each does, preview its effect, add it to your sheet, or remove one of your own from the library.
- **Background library** (on the Library). 43 backgrounds: the SRD's four in full, the other 2024 ones' feat, skills and tool, and older ones from the 2014 Player's Handbook, the Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide and Van Richten's Guide to Ravenloft with their proficiencies and a summary of their feature. **Take** one to make it your background and add it to Features & traits.
- **Spell points (optional).** In Spellcasting, switch a character from **Slots** to **Spell points**, the Dungeon Master's Guide variant: a pool by spellcaster level (half casters round up, as in the 2024 rules), each slot level costs its points (1st 2, 2nd 3, 3rd 5, 4th 6, 5th 7, 6th 9, 7th 10, 8th 11, 9th 13), no higher than your table's highest level, one slot each of 6th level and up per Long Rest, and everything back on a Long Rest. Cast offers each level you can afford; Pact Magic keeps its own slots.
- **Game states.** **Characters → Game states** saves where a session stands without the character itself: hit points, spent slots or points, used features, conditions, the enemies and the damage done to them, the initiative order, counters, companions' hit points, coins and item counts. Load one onto the character (even a newer copy, after an update or a level up; things are matched by name, and anything missing is listed), or **Save as a file** and open it on another device. Loading first saves where you are, so it can be undone. Full backups include game states too.
- **Spells by where they come from.** Each spell remembers what gave it: a class (Ranger, Warlock…) or a feat (Shadow-Touched, Magic Initiate). With more than one source, the Spellcasting panel shows them all together, each tagged, or one source at a time, and lists each source's ability, save DC and attack bonus (a Ranger's spells use Wisdom, a Warlock's Charisma, a feat's the ability it names). Spells that say nothing are matched to the one class of yours whose list has them; you can change any spell's source in its editor. Feat spells don't count against your classes' prepared spells.
- **Paste a spell, copy a spell.** Add spell → **Paste a spell** takes a spell's text (a book entry, a D&D Beyond copy, a friend's homebrew or your notes, even just a name and a description) and shows what it read: level, school, casting time, range, components, duration, how it's rolled, damage, upcasting, classes and its animation. Add it to your sheet (filed under the class of yours it lists, or the one you pick) and, optionally, to your spell library. In a spell's editor, **Fill in from pasted text** fills its fields from pasted text, changing only what the text gives. **Copy as text**, on any spell on your sheet or in the library, copies it as a rulebook-style entry followed by a few lines with its exact numbers (Roll, Damage, Higher levels, Attacks/darts/rays, Cantrip scaling, Look), so it pastes back, here or in someone else's copy of the app, exactly as you made it. Every SRD spell survives this round trip unchanged (checked by a test).
- **Your own versions of spells.** Change any spell (its damage, range, element, description), then **Save as a new spell in my library** under its own name: your version sits in the spell list beside the original, which stays as it was. **Reset to the original** undoes your changes.
- **Feats.** The **Feat library** (on the Library page, and **Feats** in Features & traits) lists every feat the app knows, the SRD 5.2.1's and yours, by kind, source and whether it gives spells. **Take** a feat and it lands on your sheet: the +1 to the ability you pick (capped at 20, or 30 for an Epic Boon), its spells (marked as coming from the feat and cast with its ability) and, for feats like Shadow-Touched, a once-per-Long-Rest free cast of each, offered in the spell's Cast menu. **Paste or upload** feats (2024 and 2014 book layouts, D&D Beyond copies, notes, or an AI's JSON) and the reader fills in the prerequisite, ability choices and granted spells for you to check. **Create a feat** or **Copy and edit** one: name, kind, prerequisite, the abilities it can raise, the spells it gives (fixed ones, and ones you choose by level, school and class list), free casts and casting ability. A feat already on your sheet (from Level up or an imported character) that gives spells it hasn't added shows **Set up its spells**, read from the library or from its own description.
- **Effects and sound.** Each effect combines an *element* (fire, cold, lightning, thunder, acid, poison, necrotic, radiant, force, psychic, slashing, piercing, bludgeoning, healing, arcane, shadow, nature) with a *motion* (bolt, ray, burst, cone, line, from above, aura, touch). Fireball flies out and explodes; Lightning Bolt cracks across the page; Ray of Frost freezes where it lands. Homebrew spells get a fitting animation by choosing an element and a motion. Combat actions (Move, Dash, Dodge, Hide…) have their own animations. All sounds are synthesized live with the Web Audio API, so there are no audio files.
- **Safe by default.** Screen flashes are capped at two per effect, spaced ≥350 ms apart and kept dim, which stays under the WCAG 2.2 limit of three flashes per second (SC 2.3.1). If your device asks for reduced motion, the sheet starts in reduced mode. Flashes, animations and sound can each be turned off.
- **Saving.** Every change autosaves. **Save** makes a save point you can load later, like a game save slot (loading one first saves where you are, so it can be undone). Backups can be saved as a file or shared, and restored on any device. If a browser's storage fills up, the oldest save points (never a character, never its newest point) make way for your changes, and the app says so.
- **Backgrounds.** Type or pick a background under **Character → Edit** to see what it gives, and add it to **Features & traits**: the SRD's Acolyte, Criminal, Sage and Soldier in full, the other 2024 backgrounds' feat, skills and tool, and Haunted One. A background that isn't listed gets an entry for you to describe.
- **Level up gives the whole level.** Pick the class and Level up adds its features and its subclass's (including the Drakewarden from Fizban's Treasury of Dragons, described in the app's own words: Bond of Fang and Scale at 7, Drake's Breath at 11, Perfected Bond at 15), works out hit points, asks for the subclass, Ability Score Improvement or feat, and offers the new cantrips and spells the level allows for that class, up to the highest level it can cast. A Drakewarden's drake keeps pace: Medium with wings at Ranger 7 and its Bite's extra 1d6 of its essence type (2d6 at 15), Large at 15. Undo last level takes all of it back.
- **Update from a sheet.** Character → **Update from a sheet** (or Import → *Update … from it instead*) reads a newer copy of the character's sheet, such as a D&D Beyond PDF after a level up, and lists what differs in sections to tick: classes and levels, ability scores, hit point maximum, saves and skills, armor, speed, languages and training, new spells, features and weapons (gear, coins, notes and removals start unticked). A save point is made first. Companions, hit points right now, spent slots and uses, conditions, enemies, counters, game states, the portrait, tiles and dice are left as they are, and new levels bring their class features as Level up does.
- **Multiple characters**, with JSON import/export.
- **Ready-made characters** under **Characters**: Anaurys (Aasimar Ranger (Drakewarden) 4 / Warlock 2, with his drake Artex, from his player's D&D Beyond sheet) and the example sorcerer, Seraphine Vale. Rules the SRD doesn't include are described in the app's own words, with the book named.

## Use it offline

There are five standalone versions. None needs an account or a server once it's on your device, and none sends your data anywhere.

| Version | Best for | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Installed app** (PWA) | Phones and tablets; desktops too | Open **https://death7310.github.io/The-Arcane-Tome-/** once while online, then **Settings → Install** (Chrome, Edge, Android) or **Share → Add to Home Screen** (iPhone/iPad, in Safari). After that first visit it runs fully offline, and updates arrive only when you tap **Update now**. |
| **Android app** (APK) | Android phones and tablets | Download `arcane-tome.apk` from the site (Settings shows the link) and open it; Android asks once to allow installs from your browser. Saves to a file in the app's own storage; backups go to **Documents › Arcane Tome** or the share sheet. |
| **iPad / iPhone app** (IPA) | iPads and iPhones, if you're willing to sideload | Apple installs apps only from the App Store unless you sign them yourself. See [On an iPad or iPhone](#on-an-ipad-or-iphone). |
| **Mac app** (DMG) | Mac laptops and desktops (Apple silicon and Intel) | Download `arcane-tome-mac.dmg` from the site (Settings shows the link on a Mac). See [On a Mac](#on-a-mac). |
| **Single file** (`arcane-tome.html`) | Laptops and desktops | Build with `npm run build:single` and open `dist-single/index.html` by double-clicking. Fonts, code and sounds are all inside the one file. |

Your characters are stored on the device you use, in the browser's storage for that version. The installed app and the single file each keep their own saves. Use **Settings → Back up all characters** regularly, and **Restore from a backup** to move to another device. Restoring never overwrites a newer local edit: for each character, the copy edited most recently wins.

On iPhone and iPad, install the app rather than using it as a bookmarked website: Safari can delete a website's stored data after 7 days without a visit, but apps added to the Home Screen are exempt ([WebKit, 2020](https://webkit.org/blog/10218/full-third-party-cookie-blocking-and-more/)).

### On an iPad or iPhone

There are three ways, from simplest to most app-like.

1. **Home Screen app (free, no computer).** In Safari open the site, tap **Share → Add to Home Screen**. It runs offline with its own icon and keeps its saves. It is the website, so a few app extras are missing: no buzz on a natural 20 (Safari has no vibration), and backups download through Safari rather than going to their own folder.
2. **The iPad app, sideloaded (free Apple ID, needs a Mac or Windows PC to set up).** This is the same app as the Android APK, built for iPadOS: backups appear in the Files app under **On My iPad › Arcane Tome**, the screen stays on during a fight, and crits buzz on iPhones (iPads have no vibration motor). Every push to `main` publishes it as `arcane-tome.ipa` on the site. Because it isn't from the App Store, a sideloading tool signs it with your own Apple ID:
   1. On a computer, install **AltServer** from [altstore.io](https://altstore.io) and use it to put **AltStore** on the iPad (or **SideStore**, a fork that can refresh apps from the iPad itself once set up).
   2. On the iPad: **Settings → Privacy & Security → Developer Mode → On**, then restart. iPadOS 16 and later require this for apps you sign yourself ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/enabling-developer-mode-on-a-device)).
   3. On the iPad, open the site in Safari, then **Settings** in the app shows **Download the app file (IPA)**. It lands in **Files › Downloads**.
   4. In AltStore: **My Apps → +**, pick `arcane-tome.ipa`.
   5. **Refresh it at least every 7 days.** With a free Apple ID, a self-signed app stops opening after 7 days until it is refreshed, and only 3 such apps can be installed at once (AltStore itself counts as one) ([AltStore FAQ](https://faq.altstore.io/altstore-classic/your-altstore)). AltStore refreshes in the background when the iPad and the computer running AltServer share a Wi-Fi network. An expired app keeps its saves; deleting the app deletes them, so back up first (**Settings → Save a backup to my iPad**).
   6. To update, install the newer `arcane-tome.ipa` the same way. It replaces the app and keeps your characters.
3. **TestFlight or the App Store (needs a paid Apple Developer account, $99 a year).** Signing then lasts a year, with no computer or weekly refresh, and TestFlight can share it with your group. This isn't set up: it needs the account's signing certificate and an App Store Connect key added to GitHub as secrets, and the CI job would sign and upload the build. App Store listing also means Apple's review.

### On a Mac

1. Download `arcane-tome-mac.dmg` (on a Mac, **Settings** on the site shows the link) and open it.
2. Drag **Arcane Tome** onto the **Applications** folder in that window.
3. Open Arcane Tome from Applications. The first time, macOS says it can't verify the app, because it isn't notarized by Apple (that needs a paid Apple Developer account). Choose **Done**, then open **System Settings → Privacy & Security**, scroll down, and choose **Open Anyway** next to Arcane Tome ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102445)). After that it opens normally.

It runs fully offline and keeps your characters in its own storage on the Mac (`~/Library/Application Support/Arcane Tome`), separate from any browser. **Save a backup file** asks where to save, starting in Documents. To update, download the newer DMG and replace the app; your characters stay.

Installing the website from Chrome (**Settings → Install Arcane Tome as an app**) also works on a Mac, and updates itself.

### Updating

**Settings → App version** shows the version and build, with **Check for updates**. The website and the installed web app fetch the new version themselves (**Update now** switches to it). The Android, iPad/iPhone and Mac apps check the site's `version.json`, which CI writes on every publish with the build number and the app files it holds; when a newer build is out (the apps also look by themselves every few hours and show a banner), **Download the update** opens the new APK, IPA or DMG in the browser. The Mac app updates itself instead: **Update and restart** downloads the new DMG, checks it against the SHA-256 in `version.json`, quits, and a small script (`desktop/updater.cjs`) swaps the new app into place and reopens it, putting the old one back if anything fails. This doesn't need Apple's paid signing, which macOS's own update framework does; CI runs the swap on a real Mac on every build. It needs the app in a folder your account can change (Applications, for an administrator); otherwise the download route is offered. Installing it over the app keeps every character: Android installs an update signed with the same key over the old app, AltStore or SideStore with the same Apple ID replaces the iPad app, and the Mac keeps its data in `~/Library/Application Support/Arcane Tome` when you replace the app in Applications.

### Hosting

Every push runs the tests and builds the website, the Android app, and the iPad/iPhone and Mac apps on a macOS runner (see `.github/workflows/deploy.yml`). Pushes to `main` publish the site to GitHub Pages at https://death7310.github.io/The-Arcane-Tome-/, with the signed APK at `arcane-tome.apk` the unsigned IPA at `arcane-tome.ipa`, and the Mac app at `arcane-tome-mac.dmg`. Anyone with the link can use the app; each person's characters stay on their own device.

One-time setup: **Settings → Pages → Source: GitHub Actions**, and a repository secret **`ANDROID_SIGNING_KEY`** (`password:base64-of-the-.p12`). Android only installs an update over an app signed with the same key, so keep a private copy of that key: if it is lost, the next version can only be installed after uninstalling the old one, which deletes its saved characters.

## Run it

```bash
npm install
npm run dev          # local dev server
npm test             # rules + dice unit tests
npm run build        # static site in dist/
npm run build:single # one self-contained HTML file in dist-single/
npm run android:sync # web build for the app + copy into android/ (then open android/ in Android Studio)
npm run build:native && npx cap sync ios  # the same for ios/ (then open ios/App/App.xcodeproj in Xcode)
```

The Android project in `android/` is a [Capacitor](https://capacitorjs.com) shell around the same web app, built with JDK 21 and the Android SDK. The iOS project in `ios/` is the same shell for iPad and iPhone; it needs a Mac with Xcode 26 or later (Capacitor 8's minimum). CI builds both on every push.

## Roadmap

The latest audit and a prioritized list of improvements are in [docs/IMPROVEMENTS.md](docs/IMPROVEMENTS.md).

1. **More systems (Call of Cthulhu 7e, World of Darkness / Chronicles).** The dice engine already handles d100 and success-counting pools. The next step is a `GameSystem` interface, extracted once a second system shows what is truly shared, rather than guessed up front. Homebrew games that keep 5e's six abilities and d20 already work through the rules library; a game with its own stats or dice needs this step.
2. **World and combat builder.** Describe a setting or a combat system in plain language, and an LLM drafts a *declarative* system definition (stats, derived formulas, dice mechanics, actions). The app validates it against a schema before running it. Generated data can be checked and safely interpreted. Generated code can't.
